    The price, and everything that didn’t move it

    Each step is an accepted bid, placed when the first subscriber heard of it. Every other mark is a bid that did not count, placed when it was sent, at the amount it offered. A mark above the line can be a bid that lost to one still being held. A square before the shaded part is a bid still undecided, or just arrived, when the auction’s time ran out: some had lost the answer to their card hold, and the close is announced a little after the end.

      Race lanes: what was in flight around it

      One lane per bid that was waiting for its reply while the selected one was being decided. The thick bar is the card hold as the recording proxy saw it. The vertical line is when the first subscriber heard the selected bid: its hold ends to the left. A bid can be held and still lose; its hold is then released.

        Did every subscriber hear the same thing?

        Each square is one subscriber hearing one version. Darker means later after the first subscriber to hear that version.

        Where the time went

        Time from when a bid was due until a reply showed its outcome. Seen from outside the target, so a wait for a database lock can’t be told apart from other work. A bid answered “pending” is polled twice a second, so its outcome is known only to within that.

        When things went wrong

        Faults were put in on purpose: some by the payment fake, some by the load generator. An instance dying, a live stream dropping and a release or capture failing for good were not injected, so this says nothing about them.
